ATS-Friendly Resume Format: A Simple 2026 Guide

Updated June 2026 · 7 min read

If you've applied to jobs online and heard nothing back, the problem may not be your experience — it may be that a machine rejected your resume before a person ever saw it. Most companies use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to filter applications. This guide gives you the exact format that gets through.

What an ATS actually does

An ATS scans your resume, extracts the text into a database, and matches it against the job description. If it can't read a section — because it's inside an image, a fancy table, or an unusual layout — that information is lost. A resume that looks gorgeous to you can arrive as garbled text to the recruiter. The goal of an ATS-friendly format is simple: be easy for the software to read while still looking professional to humans.

The 7 rules of an ATS-friendly format

1. Use a single-column layout (or a clean two-column)

Single-column is the safest because the reading order is unambiguous. Heavily designed multi-column resumes with sidebars can confuse older systems. If you use two columns, keep them simple and make sure each section has a clear heading.

2. Stick to standard section headings

Use the headings ATS expects: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills. Creative labels like "Where I've Made an Impact" look clever but may not be recognized as your work history.

3. Choose a common, readable font

Use a clean sans-serif (Inter, Arial, Calibri) or a standard serif (Georgia). Body text should be 10–12pt. Avoid decorative or condensed fonts that can render incorrectly.

4. Never put text inside images

An ATS cannot read text baked into a graphic or logo. Your name, contact details, and section content must all be real, selectable text.

5. Match keywords from the job description

The ATS ranks you on relevance. If the posting asks for "inventory management" and you have that experience, use that exact phrase. Don't keyword-stuff — just mirror the real, relevant language.

6. Keep dates and structure consistent

Use a consistent date format (e.g., "Mar 2021 – Present") and put your job title, company, and dates in a predictable order for every role. Consistency helps the parser map your history correctly.

7. Export as a text-based PDF

Save your resume as a PDF generated from real text — not a screenshot or scanned image. Modern ATS read text PDFs reliably, and the format keeps your layout intact on the recruiter's screen.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ATS-friendly resume?

One that uses a clean layout, standard headings, and a common font so Applicant Tracking Systems can parse it before a human reviews it.

What file format is best for ATS?

A text-based PDF. It's readable by modern systems and preserves your formatting everywhere.

One column or two?

Single-column is safest. A clean two-column can work, but avoid graphics, tables, and image-based text.
